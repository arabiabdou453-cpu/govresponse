The Problem

Legacy tools weren't built for this

Most emergency management teams are coordinating multi-agency disasters with software designed for a different era — or no software at all. The result is preventable chaos at the worst possible moment.

📋

Disconnected tools

Incident commanders are juggling radio calls, spreadsheets, whiteboards, and five different software systems — none of which talk to each other.

📡

Field teams flying blind

Field operators get assignments at briefing and then operate in isolation. The EOC doesn’t know what’s actually happening until someone calls it in.

⏱

Minutes cost lives

A need reported via radio, written down, entered into a system, and then assigned to a resource can take 20+ minutes. That gap is where people die.

📂

After-action is a nightmare

Reconstructing what happened, when, and who was where requires digging through radio logs, paper forms, and faded whiteboards. Lessons don’t get learned.

Minutes lost

Needs relayed by radio, written down, re-keyed, then assigned — while the EOC and the field work from different pictures.

Paper trails

Whiteboards, spreadsheets, and ICS forms filled out by hand — hard to share live and harder to reconstruct later.

After-action pain

Cal OES after-action reports and FEMA cost-recovery packets stitched together by hand from logs that were never built for it.

The Platform

One platform. Command and field, together.

GovResponse is a single web application — no native app to install, no second system to sync. The command view and the field run on the same real-time data. What the EOC sees, the field gets. What the field reports, the EOC acts on — instantly.

Capabilities

Built for real incident conditions

Every feature in GovResponse was designed with one constraint in mind: it has to work when the stakes are highest and conditions are worst.

🗺

Live Tactical Map

A Mapbox operations map with resources, needs, shelters, evacuation zones, weather, and damage — updating in real time across every connected user.

🚑

Resources & Needs

Deploy and track units by category and status, log operational needs with urgency and population, and assign resources with live progress tracking.

🏨

Shelters & Mass Care

Register shelters with capacity, intake and discharge, per-shelter supplies, and ADA, pet, and medical flags — with occupancy on the map.

🏘

Evacuation Management

Manage evacuation zones with status from advisory to order to repopulation, plus door-to-door address checks for the field — pending, clear, occupied, no access.

📑

Built-in ICS Forms

Generate print-ready ICS 201, 202, 203, 214, and 209 — auto-populated from live incident data and the activity log. Save to PDF in a click.

🏛

California SEMS

Elect NIMS or NIMS + SEMS. Map ICS sections to SEMS EOC functions and work the state cost-recovery certification checklist by organizational level.

📊

After-Action Reports

The six narrative sections required by CCR Title 19 §2450, an operational rollup, and a 90-day Cal OES deadline tracker — exportable for the record.

💵

FEMA Cost Recovery

The 2025 FEMA equipment rate codebook is built in. Auto-calculate cost from deployment to release, capture demob sign-offs, and roll it up for reimbursement.

🏚

Damage Assessment

Log FEMA preliminary damage assessments by severity, category, and ownership with cost estimates, mapped and rolled up into the ICS 209.

⛈

Live Weather (NWS)

Active National Weather Service alerts and forecast for the incident location, color-coded by severity on the map and refreshed automatically.

✏️

GIS & Map Overlays

Draw hazards, closures, and zones with polygon, line, and point tools that persist across operational periods — and import reference GeoJSON or KML layers.

📡

Offline-First

A progressive web app that keeps working without signal. Edits queue locally and replay on reconnect — no native install, no app store, any device.
